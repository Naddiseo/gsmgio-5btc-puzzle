#### Historical Note

In 1933, the Emergency Banking Act and subsequent legislation ended the redeemability of Federal Reserve notes in gold.
By January 30, 1934, Federal Reserve notes were no longer redeemable in gold.

Executive Order 11110, issued by President John F. Kennedy in 1963, 
amended Executive Order 10289, originally issued by President Harry S. Truman in 1951.

Among other things, EO 11110 delegated to the Secretary of the Treasury certain presidential authority concerning 
the issuance of silver certificates and the coinage of silver currency.

By 1963, the Federal Reserve began issuing $1 Federal Reserve notes as a replacement for $1 silver certificates, 
which were being phased out because of increasing demand for silver coinage.

Thus, domestically, Federal Reserve notes no longer required direct redeemability in gold or silver, 
although the monetary system still retained important statutory relationships with gold and silver.

In 1971, President Richard Nixon suspended the convertibility of U.S. dollars into gold for foreign official holders
, effectively ending the dollar's role as the gold-convertible anchor of the Bretton Woods system.

This marked the beginning of the end of the Bretton Woods monetary system and the transition toward the modern fiat-dollar system.
